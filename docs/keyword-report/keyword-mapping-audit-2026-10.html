Task 2 — Why the map is limiting clicks

  • The map is built on a demand floor that is too low. 244 of 538 products (45%) have a primary with no measurable volume, and the median product primary is 50 searches/month. Even ranking #1 for it yields a handful of clicks. Only 125 products have a primary above 300.
  • Head terms are everywhere and nowhere. “japanese whiskey” appears on 133 pages, “smirnoff vodka” 129, “tequila” 114, “whisky” 105, “scotch” 104 — as secondaries and tags. None is the primary of a page built to win it. Of 430 keywords with KD ≤ 28 and 1,000+ volume, 347 are not a primary anywhere: tequila 22,200, baileys 22,200, prosecco 22,200, champagne 18,100, veuve clicquot 14,800, whisky 14,800, gin 14,800, smirnoff vodka 12,100, jim beam 9,900, kahlua 9,900 and more.
  • The pages that could win those terms target softer variants. Mezcal, Prosecco, Cider, Rum, Soju, Rosé, Sauvignon blanc and Mixers collections use primaries of 50–110 searches (“cora prosecco” 50, “somersby cider bottle” 70, “bottle of malibu rum” 70, “soju buy” 50, “water prices” 110) while their own head terms are 1,600–22,000.
  • Cannibalisation clusters. Four vodka collections target “buy / for sale / sale” vodka; /shop/spirit/, Spiced rum and White rum all target “buy rum / rum for sale”; Pink gin’s “gin sale” is the same SERP as the Gin collection’s “buy gin online”; White and Gold tequila both use “buy tequila”; Scotch, the Whisky hub, Irish, /shop/ and /contact/ share the “whisky / whiskey sale” SERP; 3 product groups share an identical primary. Google rotates which URL it shows, so none builds authority.
  • Intent mismatch on non-sales pages. /contact/ targets “whiskey for sale”, /about/ “buy single malt whisky online”, /shipping/ “wine delivery”, /blog/ “american whiskey”. They compete with collections for transactional queries a contact or about page can’t satisfy.
  • The homepage splits its signal six ways. Six unrelated primaries (Glenfiddich, pinot grigio, Corona, Kraken, Don Julio, Premix King). Each is also the natural primary of a collection that is currently mapped to something smaller.
  • Secondary sets don’t match their pages. 12 collections have 0–1 of 5 top secondaries related to the collection (Grenache and Prosecco carry low-carb-beer terms, Absinthe carries Baileys and Sambuca, Baijiu carries coffee/orange liqueur, Port wine carries cider). All 24 Ice premix flavours reuse the same secondary set.
  • Blogs sit at the wrong end of the funnel. 7 blog primaries are labelled Commercial/Transactional by Semrush (Absolut vodka 8,100, Macallan whisky 1,000, Nikka whiskey 1,300…), pitting them against your own collections; 5 are 8,000–40,500-search head terms (cider 40,500; ginger beer 9,900; vermouth 9,900) where publishers and large retailers typically hold the results; 3 have no volume at all.
  • The blog plan would add more overlap. 13 of the 36 queued posts already match an existing post’s primary (baileys irish, ipa beer, australian beer, shiraz wine, don julio blanco…).
  • Funnel bridges are missing. There is no gift hub even though “whisky gift pack”, “whiskey gift” and “gin gifts” (each ~2,400, KD ≤ 12) are among the easiest commercial wins, and no brand collections for Smirnoff, Absolut, Jack Daniels, Jim Beam, Kraken, Moet or Veuve Clicquot despite 8,000–18,000 searches each.
  • Tags don’t help rankings. The 20 tags per page are emitted as meta keywords and JSON-LD; Google ignores the former and treats the latter as a weak signal (your own audit says the same). They are not a substitute for a dedicated, uniquely-targeted URL.
What the map can’t explain, and how to check. A site that earned clicks and then dropped to ~0 for a month usually suffered an indexing, quality or sitewide event rather than a keyword choice. Technical checks on the live site look clean (HTTP 200, robots.txt allows crawling, your 529-URL audit shows 0 failures). In GSC check: (1) Performance → compare the 28 days before the drop by page — did impressions vanish sitewide (an event) or fall page-by-page (ranking)? (2) Indexing → Pages: count of “Crawled – currently not indexed” / “Discovered – not indexed” against the 529 sitemap URLs. (3) Security & Manual actions, plus Crawl stats for a sudden fetch drop. If (1) shows a sitewide cliff or (2) shows most URLs unindexed, fix that first — the redesign below then decides what comes back.
